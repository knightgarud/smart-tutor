# Subject: Electronic Devices and Circuits (B.Tech ECE Core)

## Topic 1: Semiconductor Materials (Intrinsic and Extrinsic)

A Semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator. Silicon (Si) and Germanium (Ge) are commonly used semiconductor materials. Their conductivity can be controlled by adding impurities through a process called Doping. Semiconductors are classified into Intrinsic Semiconductors (pure materials) and Extrinsic Semiconductors (materials containing added impurities). Extrinsic semiconductors are further divided into P-Type and N-Type semiconductors.

## Topic 2: PN Junction Diode and Its Biasing

A PN Junction Diode is a two-terminal semiconductor device formed by joining P-type and N-type semiconductor materials. It allows current to flow mainly in one direction and blocks it in the opposite direction. When forward biased, the depletion region becomes narrow and current flows easily. In reverse bias, the depletion region becomes wider and only a very small leakage current flows. The typical cut-in voltage is 0.7 V for Silicon and 0.3 V for Germanium diodes.

## Topic 3: Rectifiers (Half-Wave and Full-Wave)

A Rectifier is an electronic circuit that converts Alternating Current (AC) into Direct Current (DC) using semiconductor diodes. The main types are Half-Wave Rectifier, Full-Wave Centre-Tapped Rectifier, and Bridge Rectifier. A half-wave rectifier uses only one half-cycle of AC, whereas a full-wave rectifier utilizes both half-cycles. Rectifiers are commonly used in DC power supplies, battery chargers, televisions, and electronic equipment.

## Topic 4: Zener Diode and Voltage Regulation

A Zener Diode is a specially designed semiconductor diode that operates in the reverse breakdown region. It maintains an approximately constant voltage across its terminals even when the input voltage or load current changes within its operating limits. The voltage at which the diode enters breakdown is called Zener Voltage (Vz). It is widely used in voltage regulation, reference voltage circuits, and overvoltage protection systems.

## Topic 5: Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

A Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device used for amplification and switching of electrical signals. It consists of three terminals: Emitter, Base, and Collector. The two main types are NPN and PNP transistors. A small base current controls a larger collector current. A BJT operates in three major regions: Cut-off Region (OFF state), Active Region (amplification), and Saturation Region (ON state).

## Topic 6: Transistor Configurations (CB, CE, and CC)

A transistor configuration describes how the terminals of a transistor are connected in an electronic circuit. There are three main configurations: Common Base (CB), Common Emitter (CE), and Common Collector (CC). Common Base provides low input resistance, Common Emitter provides high current and voltage gain, and Common Collector provides high input resistance and low output resistance. These configurations are widely used in amplifier and signal-processing circuits.

## Topic 7: Field Effect Transistor (FET and MOSFET)

A Field Effect Transistor (FET) is a voltage-controlled semiconductor device in which an electric field controls the flow of current through a conducting channel. It has three terminals: Gate, Source, and Drain. The two major types are JFET (Junction Field Effect Transistor) and MOSFET (Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect Transistor). FETs are widely used in switching circuits, amplifiers, microprocessors, memory devices, and digital electronic systems.

## Topic 8: Operational Amplifier (Op-Amp)

An Operational Amplifier is a high-gain electronic voltage amplifier that amplifies the difference between two input signals. It has two input terminals: Inverting (-) and Non-Inverting (+), and one output terminal. An ideal Op-Amp has infinite voltage gain, infinite input impedance, zero output impedance, and infinite bandwidth. Its applications include inverting amplifiers, non-inverting amplifiers, voltage comparators, integrators, and differentiators.

## Topic 9: Feedback Amplifiers and Oscillators

A Feedback Amplifier is an electronic circuit in which a portion of the output signal is returned to the input. Feedback is classified into Positive Feedback and Negative Feedback. Negative feedback improves stability, reduces distortion, and increases bandwidth, whereas positive feedback is commonly used in oscillator circuits. An Oscillator is an electronic circuit that generates continuous periodic AC signals without requiring an external periodic input signal. Common types include RC Phase Shift, Wien Bridge, Hartley, Colpitts, and Crystal Oscillators.

## Topic 10: Electronic Filters and Their Applications

An Electronic Filter is a circuit designed to allow specific frequency signals to pass while reducing or rejecting unwanted frequencies. Filters are generally constructed using resistors, capacitors, inductors, and operational amplifiers. The four main types are Low-Pass Filter (LPF), High-Pass Filter (HPF), Band-Pass Filter (BPF), and Band-Stop Filter (BSF). They are widely used in audio systems, communication devices, power supplies, and noise-reduction circuits.
